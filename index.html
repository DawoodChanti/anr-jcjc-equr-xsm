01 — THE PROJECT

How reliable is a prediction?
And its explanation?

Project overview

EQUR-XSM — Evidential Quantification of Uncertainty for Reliability in XAI Saliency Maps investigates how medical-image AI can communicate both its predictions and their reliability. It combines uncertainty estimation, confidence calibration and visual explanations, with applications in classification and segmentation. Led by Dawood Al Chanti at GIPSA-lab, this ANR JCJC project runs from October 2025 to October 2029.

Scientific summary

A medical-image model can produce an incorrect prediction with high confidence. A visual explanation alone does not resolve this problem: highlighting a region does not establish that the explanation is reliable. EQUR-XSM addresses these connected challenges by studying uncertainty in predictions and in the saliency maps used to explain them.

The project builds on Evidential Deep Learning (EDL) to estimate uncertainty and improve confidence calibration. It also investigates saliency maps enriched with pixel-wise uncertainty, aiming to provide information about influential image regions and the reliability of their interpretation.

A further objective is to combine complementary imaging modalities, such as MRI and CT, while accounting for the uncertainty associated with each input. The programme extends from image classification to single-modality and multimodal segmentation, targeting applications involving tumours, skeletal muscle abnormalities and haematological diseases.

Planned evaluation includes open datasets and clinical collaboration with the university hospitals of Grenoble and Nantes. The intended contribution is a set of methods for better-calibrated predictions, uncertainty-aware explanations and more reliable multimodal analysis. These are research objectives; clinical benefit remains to be established.

Project descriptions: ANR · GIPSA-lab.

The research programme addresses three complementary questions.

01.1

Calibrated confidence

Develop uncertainty estimates that better reflect prediction reliability, with the aim of limiting errors associated with overconfidence.

01.2

Uncertainty in explanations

Enrich saliency maps with pixel-level uncertainty to indicate both influential image regions and the reliability of their interpretation.

01.3

Multimodal segmentation

Model uncertainty specific to each imaging modality, such as MRI or CT, when combining information for segmentation.

Target applications include tumours, skeletal muscle abnormalities and haematological diseases. The project description envisages clinical validation with the university hospitals of Grenoble and Nantes, and the use of open data to support reproducibility.

ACCEPTED RESEARCH PROGRAMME

From calibrated predictions to multimodal segmentation

The accepted proposal organises the scientific work into two connected work packages, supported by project coordination (WP0). Month ranges below describe the planned schedule.

WP1

Months 0–36

Uncertainty, calibration and evidence-based explanations

WP1.1: investigate uncertainty-aware training objectives, adaptive priors and calibration strategies, including approaches to hyperparameter optimisation.

WP1.2: investigate evidence-based saliency maps and uncertainty-aware recalibration of visual explanations.

WP2

Months 30–48

Pixel-wise uncertainty and modality-aware fusion

Extend the framework to single-modality and multimodal segmentation. Investigate how local uncertainty can guide the contribution of each modality to the fused prediction.

Three application settings

A1

Image classification

Assess predictive uncertainty, calibration and visual explanations in medical-image classification.

A2

Single-modality segmentation

Estimate uncertainty at the pixel level, with skeletal muscle analysis among the proposed clinical applications.

A3

Multimodal segmentation

Study evidential fusion and modality-specific reliability for complementary imaging inputs.

Work packages and application settings: accepted EQUR-XSM proposal (ANR JCJC 2025).

Research objectives, not demonstrated clinical outcomes. Sources: ANR project record · GIPSA-lab project page.

RESEARCH RESULT · EXPLAINS 2026

Better lesion localisation does not establish greater faithfulness.

In this brain-tumour MRI case study, Contrastive Grad-CAM Consistency (CGC) yields higher mean localisation scores than a cross-entropy baseline. The perturbation-based faithfulness metrics disagree: insertion favours CGC, deletion favours the baseline, and IROF is unchanged at the reported precision.

Study: 3,064 MRI slices from 233 patients · three tumour classes · single-channel ResNet-50 · five-fold patient-grouped cross-validation · fixed training seed.

What this result supports

Report transformation consistency, localisation and model reliance as separate evaluation targets. Improvement in one does not establish improvement in the others.

Scope: one 2-D MRI dataset, one backbone, one attribution method and one training seed. Transformation consistency was not measured quantitatively. Faithfulness scores depend on the perturbation protocol; the dataset has documented non-tumour cues. This is a methodological case study, not clinical validation or a new XAI method.

PHD RESEARCH · 2025–2028

Amrin Akter

PICSA team · GIPSA-lab · Grenoble INP – UGA

Toward Faithful and Accurate Post-hoc Visual Explanations in Medical Imaging

This PhD is part of the EQUR-XSM ANR project. It addresses the instability and unreliability of existing post-hoc explainable AI (XAI) methods, with a focus on saliency-based explanations.

The research will begin with a systematic review and comparison of existing methods, using established metrics to evaluate faithfulness, localisation, consistency, sensitivity and stability.

Based on this evaluation, the PhD aims to develop a new explanation method that is faithful by construction: its attributions should reflect the features that actually contributed to the model’s prediction during inference. The aim is to explain the classifier’s behaviour, without forcing explanations to highlight clinically relevant regions, such as tumours, that the classifier did not use.

First-author position paper accepted at EXPLAINS 2026 ↗
